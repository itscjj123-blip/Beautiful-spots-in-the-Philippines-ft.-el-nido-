About the Place

The Nest of Palawan

El Nido takes its name from the Spanish word for "the nest" — swiftlets build edible nests in the cliffs of its marble karst formations, prized in Chinese cuisine. Located on the northern tip of Palawan, this protected marine reserve shelters turquoise lagoons, hidden beaches, coral gardens, and one of the most dramatic seascapes on Earth.

Mission

To promote responsible, community-based tourism that protects El Nido's reefs and karst ecosystems for generations to come.

Vision

A world where travelers and locals thrive together — where every visit leaves the islands cleaner, richer, and more alive than before.

History

Inhabited for millennia by the Cuyonon and Tagbanwa peoples, El Nido grew from a fishing village into a globally celebrated destination, topping "best island in the world" lists year after year.

Attractions

Must-See Wonders

From hidden lagoons to powdery twin beaches — these are the places that fill photo albums and change bucket lists.

Big & Small Lagoon

Paddle through emerald gateways into cathedral-like lagoons ringed by 250-million-year-old limestone cliffs.

Nacpan Beach

Four kilometers of golden sand lined with coconut palms — regularly voted one of Asia's best beaches.

Taraw Cliff

El Nido's signature karst spire. Brave the via ferrata climb for a sunrise panorama over Bacuit Bay.

Matinloc Shrine

A hilltop shrine on Matinloc Island with sweeping views — and a mystery behind its abandoned construction.

Hidden Beach

A sliver of white sand sealed behind towering rock walls — swim through a narrow opening to find it.

Twin Beaches (Nacpan-Calitang)

Two beaches meeting at a sandbar — watch fishermen haul nets as the sun dips into the sea.

Food & Culture

Taste the Islands

Fresh catch, Cuyonon heritage, and flavors shaped by the sea.

Kinilaw

The Filipino ceviche — fresh tanigue or tuna cured in vinegar, ginger, and calamansi, served beachside.

Curacha & Lobster

Deep-sea crabs and Palawan lobsters, steamed or smothered in coconut-ginger sauce.

Lechon & Cashew Delights

Crispy roast pork for fiestas — and Palawan's famous cashew brittle and butter to take home.

Cuyonon Traditions

Songs, dances, and boat-building skills passed down for centuries among the islands' first peoples.

Handwoven Crafts

Baskets, mats, and textiles woven from pandan and nito, sold in El Nido town's market lanes.

Fiesta Spirit

Streets come alive for patron saint feasts — lechon, music, and dancing until late.

Travel Guide

How to Get There

Two routes in: over the sea and through the air. Both end in paradise.

✈️

Fly Direct

Direct flights from Manila to El Nido (Lio Airport) take about an hour. From Puerto Princesa, fly or ride north.

🚌

Overland Van

Air-conditioned vans run the 5–6 hour scenic route from Puerto Princesa through Palawan's countryside to El Nido town.

⛴️

Ferry Option

Ferries and fastcrafts connect El Nido with Coron — a beautiful 4-hour cruise across the Sulu Sea.

Travel Tips

  • Best months: November to May (dry season)
  • June–October: fewer crowds, occasional rain
  • Book island tours early in high season
  • Bring reef-safe sunscreen and dry bags
  • Carry cash — ATMs are limited in town
  • Respect the karst: no climbing on fragile cliffs
  • Try the "no single-use plastic" habit
  • Allocate at least 3–4 full days

Events

Festivals & Celebrations

Mark your calendar — El Nido knows how to throw a party.

February 22

Kalugtan Festival — El Nido's grand fiesta honoring St. Francis of Assisi, with street dancing, flotillas, and pageants.

Holy Week

Island Pilgrimages — Visita Iglesia by boat to island chapels, a uniquely El Nido tradition.

May 1–7

Feast of the Fisherfolk — Regattas, banca races, and thanksgiving feasts in fishing villages.

December

Christmas by the Sea — Lantern-lit beaches, caroling, and seafood-laden Noche Buena celebrations.
